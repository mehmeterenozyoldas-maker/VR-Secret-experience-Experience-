# Parametric Scandinavian Architecture

## Design Philosophy
Continuing from the foundations of Scandinavian brutalism (raw concrete, light wood, minimalist shapes), we introduce **Parametric Design** principles. Parametric architecture relies on algorithms and mathematical curves to create organic, continuous forms out of discrete elements. This perfectly bridges the naturalistic warmth of Nordic design with the computational, algorithm-driven nature of the digital artworks and soundscapes in this space.

## Key Architectural Updates

### 1. Undulating Parametric Timber Canopy
The flat, uniform slatted ceiling is transformed into a flowing, wave-like structure.
- **Implementation**: Instead of identical vertical placement, each wooden slat's `y` position and thickness are modulated using sine waveforms based on its spatial index. 
- **Experience**: The ceiling feels like an inverted rolling terrain or a physical manifestation of a slow-moving sound wave, guiding the eye dynamically through the gallery while preserving the warmth of the ash/birch wood.

### 2. Sweeping Ribbed Partitions (The "Acoustic Baffle" Effect)
The stark concrete partition walls are now complemented or replaced by curved, ribbed wooden walls. 
- **Implementation**: Using mathematical functions (like Archimedean spirals or overlapping sine waves) to position vertical wooden ribs `(0.1m x 4m x 0.3m)`. 
- **Experience**: These ribs act as spatial dividers that change in opacity depending on the viewing angle. When viewed straight on, they seem transparent; when viewed at an angle, they form a solid organic mass. This adds a layer of kinetic discovery as the user movies via XR.

### 3. Parametric Pedestal Integration
Instead of simple cubic concrete pedestals, the bases holding the computational art pieces incorporate slight geometric twists or cascading step patterns—softening the harsh modernist lines with computational logic. Only slight adjustments are made here to keep the focus on the main artworks.

## Synergy with Computational Art
By integrating these parametric physical structures, the gallery's "real" architecture echoes the generative, mathematical logic of the virtual exhibits (such as the swirling particle systems and algorithmic audio engines). The VR space becomes a cohesive, holistic environment where both the architecture and the art are born from computation.
